01

The problem and insight

Who it affects, what happens today, and why it's broken

Who

Leisure travellers and group organisers (18–35 years)

People who book flights, hotels and activities, often for trips with friends, family or college groups.

What happens today

They book a flight or hotel, then later find a cheaper price for the same dates. They check cancellation policies by hand, compare new prices, work out possible refunds, and discuss options across several platforms and people.

Why it's broken

  • Policies are hard to interpret and differ across platforms.
  • You have to check several websites and the fine print.
  • The total cost impact (after fees, refunds, etc.) is unclear.
  • Getting a group to agree takes time.
  • It's easy to make the wrong decision and lose money.

Key insight

“Travellers don't just want to know if a new price is lower. They want to know if it's actually worth switching, for the entire trip, with clear next steps.”

    06

    What I'd test next

    The riskiest assumption, how I'll test it, and what success looks like

    Riskiest assumption

    Prices and availability may change between analysis and checkout, making the recommendation stale and the expected saving disappear.

    Even if the AI finds a cheaper option, the fare may rise or become unavailable by the time the user tries to rebook. That would make for a poor experience and lose their trust.

    Test plan

    1. Run 20–30 repeated fare checks across a small set of routes, airlines and time intervals (e.g. every 30–60 mins).
    2. Compare the displayed fare with the actual fare at checkout.
    3. Record price freshness, recommendation accuracy and how often savings persist.
    4. Test how the product handles price changes, unavailable fares and policy updates (e.g. clear alerts and alternative options).
